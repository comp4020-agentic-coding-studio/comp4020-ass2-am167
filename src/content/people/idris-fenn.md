Idris came out of transport modelling: six years at a consultancy building
demand models for outer-suburban corridors, which they describe as "being paid
to tell people their arterial widening would not work, and then watching it get
built anyway." They teach Weeks 2, 5 and 6 — the block pattern that sets the
network's shape, the transit spine, and the peak-load stress test, which is
their design.

They are the tutor most likely to make you undo something. Their standing
position is that the first network you draw is a diagram of your assumptions
rather than a plan, and that the fastest way to find out which assumption is
wrong is to run it at 8am and watch where it fails.

If you arrive at their desk crit with a one-way conversion as your fix, bring the
before-and-after numbers. They will ask for them.
