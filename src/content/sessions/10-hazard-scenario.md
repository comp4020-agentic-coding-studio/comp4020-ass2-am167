## Before you come

Bring `<student ID>-w09` exactly as submitted, together with last week’s trend
readings. Check the fire-hazard and electricity views over your district, and
read wind off the arrows inside the electricity view — there is no wind view to
open on its own. Bring the Week 3 zoning drawing with the **Pellow cells still
industrial**, the Week 7 reverse-salient card and the unfunded treatment line.

Have the issued `kerrow-ref-w10-d0`, `-d3`, `-d6` and `-recovered` checkpoints
available. Before opening any of them, branch the personal district as
`<student ID>-w10-pre`. There is no 19-day live run.

## The load case

The supported load case is a **forest fire beginning on forested Verrall
Ridge**, the Week 1 rejected site. It tests the only westward gradient at
Thackray Cut, power transmission and the remaining fire-response radius. The
checkpoints were recorded on the design-lab build and are read as staged
reference evidence. This is not a flood or a water-source editor trick.

Ombersley remains industrial ground. Smoke and downwind load are read against
the Pellow cells as industry, not against a reference housing conversion. If a
student’s own save contains housing there, that is evidence about that save,
not a replacement for the common scenario.

## The three hours

Begin by stacking the fire-hazard view, the electricity view's transmission
lines and wind arrows, and the Thackray Cut geometry. Draw the fire-exposed
land and count what sits inside and outside the response radius. Then load d0,
d3 and d6 in order and use the same 08:00 observation protocol on each.

In the reference sequence, Thackray Cut is closed for **four in-game days** and
peak travel reaches **13.6 minutes** against Week 9’s 11.1-minute trend. The d3
electricity availability bar shows the power shortfall, and the fire-coverage
view shows the coupled response limit. Keep those as two fields. District-wide
happiness troughs at **41% on d3**, including in blocks that did not burn. The
event month closes at **−₡14,200**, not a continuing monthly rate.

At 14:40, load `-recovered`. No intervention is inserted between d6 and the
recovery trace. Save that checkpoint out as `<student ID>-w10`: your own
district was never run through the fire, so the issued recovered state is what
stands as your Week 10 record, and Week 11 plots its land-value row. Your
untouched pre-event district stays as `-w10-pre`, and the two are read
side by side, never merged.

Finish with two board columns and then cost, in writing, whether to withdraw
fire-exposed land or defend it with access, power and response capacity. Do not
build that choice this afternoon.

## Broke, or looked alarming

**What broke:** ridge access, power transmission and fire response. Each entry
names the view, reading and checkpoint that supports it. These failures connect
to siting and network decisions made in Weeks 1, 3 and 7.

**What looked alarming:** the happiness trough and notification cluster. Both
resolve on the recovered checkpoint without a design move. That does not make
them false; it makes them event readings rather than evidence of the district’s
settled state.

Withdraw exposed land and lose rate base. Defend it and carry the exposure plus
the cost of oversizing. The trade is **fiscal performance against livability**.
There is no branch in which both improve.

## The quoting rule

**Event ≠ trend.** A later claim never says “happiness was 41%” on its own. It
says “41% on `kerrow-ref-w10-d3`” and sets that against `-recovered`. Keep the
land-value row blank this week; Week 11 plots Week 9 to Week 11 with the gap
dashed.

Leave with `<student ID>-w10` saved from the recovered checkpoint, `-w10-pre`
from before any checkpoint was opened, the two-column fault sheet, three named
screenshots and the costed exposure statement. Copy both saves off-machine and
upload the record by 21:00.
