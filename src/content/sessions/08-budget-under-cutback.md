## The cut

Remove **22% of the worksheet’s total eligible allocated monthly service
outlay**, once. The denominator is printed on the issued sheet and excludes tax
moves and capital works. The amount is imposed. The composition is yours, and
that composition is the design problem.

The worksheet allocates citywide category costs to the district; it is not a
native district budget. The issued transit-heavy, coverage-heavy and balanced
checkpoints apply whole-city controls, so any mismatch between your paper
closure and the available control is part of the record.

## Before you come

Bring the unmodified Week 7 personal save, the three reference branches and the
returned reverse-salient card. Keep both utility increments costed and ordered;
the exercise does not assume either was built. Total the eligible outlay before
13:00 and open the fiscal and land-value views.

Seal one prediction before any slider moves: which line you expect to cut, and
which of **fiscal performance**, **mobility performance** or **livability** you
expect it to cost. The desk crit compares that sentence with a settled branch,
and the difference is evidence rather than an error to hide.

## Line zero

The cheapest closure is to leave both Week 7 utility increments unfunded. At
13:10 the room costs that option properly, reads the water, sewage and
pollution evidence, then refuses it. The first entry on the corridor budget
sheet instead allocates spend to the water-production and sewage-treatment
increments, protecting them from the cut.

Everything else is chosen inside that fence. Reducing the bus fleet from four
to three saves an allocated **₡1,200/month** and produces a calculated headway
of **8 minutes**: 24-minute round trip ÷ 3 buses. A roughly 10-minute observed
headway is branch-specific if the round trip lengthened. Setting the citywide
parks and recreation category to 60% is likewise a branch control, not an
Ockley Green building setting.

## In the room

Pairs first compose the closure on paper: line, current monthly figure, cut
figure, saving and the named metric that carries it. Only then map it to an
issued branch, take its 08:00 readings and open the prediction at the desk crit.

At 15:05, the roster speaks or submits each line in a fixed form while the room
does not respond:

> *“I have cut ⟨line⟩ from ⟨figure⟩ to ⟨figure⟩ a month. What I have chosen
> not to fund is ⟨item⟩. The metric that is paying for it is ⟨fiscal
> performance / mobility performance / livability⟩, and this week it reads
> ⟨figure⟩.”*

Tobias enters the line on the shared **corridor budget sheet** while you enter
the same sentence on your signed **unfunded list**. Spoken, written, recorded or
seated equivalents use the same evidence and clock. The final read-back is
continuous and receives no comment.

## What you take away

Keep `<student ID>-w08` and the named reference branch as separate files. Leave
with the branch-labelled readings, signed unfunded list, your corridor budget
column, the opened prediction annotated where it was wrong, and three captioned
screenshots. Upload by 21:00. Next Monday’s public-realm plan must fund every
monthly line from this column and keep the ₡5,100 treatment increment visible
where it remains unfunded.

Any monthly surplus left after the cut accrues toward an uncommitted capital
reserve rather than spending itself. Note what it is not yet spent on: the
proposed **Kerrow cross-town link**, an east–west connection from the corridor
to the Pellow cells, is the standing candidate. That decision is not made this
week — it returns as a live question at the Week 12 jury.
