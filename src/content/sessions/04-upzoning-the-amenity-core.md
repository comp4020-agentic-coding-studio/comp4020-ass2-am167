## The jury

This afternoon is a jury, not a build session. Sixteen plans occupy the long
wall and two concurrent bays run from 14:00. Marisol Quaye chairs one; Sunniva
Marek, who has not watched the work develop, sits at the other. Each plan has
six minutes: two to present and four for questions. Two rounds of eight finish
before a recess, when the room leaves so the jurors can read the wall as one
body of work. The afternoon closes with Marek’s response and a metric round.

## Before 13:00

The assessment upload closes at 12:00. Use the next hour to hang in roster
order, not to revise the artefact. Put the plan at the top of the bay, the
required views beneath it, and a legible card at the bottom stating the cell’s
allocated monthly cost and return in ₡. Work not on the wall at 13:00 is marked
from the noon upload alone: the criteria still apply, but the jury cannot ask
the questions that a defence would answer.

## What was upzoned before 12:00

The week’s production happened before the deadline. You upzoned the Pelling
Cross frontage and tested the clinic on disposable three-cell branches. Keep
three measures separate:

- service coverage is the radius shown by the live UI with the building
  selected;
- walking catchment is distance along the issued network sheet;
- the clinic’s one-third cost is a worksheet allocation, not a native district
  budget.

The mixed-use core recovers mobility and raises mean land value, while its noise
lands on nearby dwellings. Read the normalised land-value score separately at
the core and the cell edge. Also check that through movement remains on the
Kerrow corridor boundary rather than using interior streets. None of these
branches is run after 13:00, and nothing discovered in the jury changes the
submitted artefact.

## The readings

The reference row is 6,900 residents in 2,650 households, +₡2,100/month, a
5.9-minute timed route, transit 0%, walk share 34%, happiness 69%, and district
land value 31. The land-value sheet carries two further rows: **48 at Pelling
Cross** and **19 at the cell edge**.

**Livability moved the wrong way.** Happiness fell from 72% to 69%, even as
land value rose from 24 to 31. Both are livability readings; quoting either one
alone reports half a metric. The 48/19 gradient is not noise in the evidence.
It is the spatial result the plan must explain.

## The tradeoff

This is **livability against fiscal performance**, made through the clinic.
Inside the cell, it sits within the intended walk but carries its full upkeep.
Shared on the Kerrow corridor, the cell pays roughly one third and the service
radius is centred on none of the three cells. Upzoning can close the same
shortfall by adding rate-paying residents, at a cost visible in core noise and
happiness.

Neither move is free. **A plan claiming all three metrics improved has not been
read carefully.** At the metric round, name the traded pair and point to the
element on the wall where it is visible.
