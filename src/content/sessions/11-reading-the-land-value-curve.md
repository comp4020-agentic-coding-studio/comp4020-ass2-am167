## Plotting the curve

Plot the district mean by hand on the sampling sheet’s 0–100 scale:
**14, 17, 24, 31, 34, 33, 34, 31, 36, —, 39** (reference-scenario values from
the pack; reconstruct a missing row from your own save, not the whole series).
Leave Week 10 as a gap and draw the Week 9–11 segment dashed.

On the same axes, add the last four blocks at the southern end of the Kerrow
corridor: **12 in Week 1 and 61 today** (reference-scenario values from the
pack, or the matching count from your own save). The district mean rose from
14 to 39, or 179%; the corridor blocks rose by a factor of five. Today creates
`<student ID>-w11`, the eleventh weekly save.

## What the curve is associated with

Read the rise as an achievement first. Land value responds to service coverage,
transit access, parks, plazas and density, and falls with noise and pollution.
Those are inputs the district installed with public money. The curve is a useful
ledger of the capital programme.

At the board, annotate each segment with the studio move it is **temporally
associated** with: separation, the Pelling Cross core, the bus spine, widening,
or the Ockley Green sequence. Write “hypothesis” or a question mark where the
connection is not supported. Several changes occurred together; the curve does
not isolate a single cause.

## What it does not record

Open the land-value view over the last four corridor blocks beside the household
and rent readouts. The overlay assigns a value to every tile but no owner to any
of them. The model cannot say who captured the uplift created by the district’s
investment.

Compare the Week 4 Pelling Cross household mix with Week 11. Record the two
counts and the difference. The model may show some homelessness, but it does
not record every household replaced as rent rises, nor exclusionary
displacement or displacement pressure. A count of the people present now is not
a history of those who could no longer remain. Unzoned land staying empty is
also a limit on what the save can say about unmet housing need.

Then write the Wickstead objection. Who might object to the Week 6 widening,
on what grounds and at what stage? The save contains no notice, hearing, vote,
appeal or owner with an interest. This does not invalidate the traffic reading;
it limits the planning claim that reading can support.

## The four blocks

Choose whether to hold the four medium-density corridor blocks or upzone them
inside the existing transit catchment. Upzoning can raise ridership and the
model’s livability reading while replacing the population over which livability
is computed. Holding them declines a measurable mobility gain and leaves the
spine subsidy in place.

Write the choice by hand. Name the metric, its direction and the population the
decision moves for. Either position is defensible; an unrecorded trade is not.
