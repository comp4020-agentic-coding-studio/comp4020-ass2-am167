## The deadline and the wall

The artefact is due at 12:00. Hang in roster order from 13:00; the wall closes
at 13:25 and the jury begins at 14:00. This is a jury, not three hours in which
to finish the work.

If the artefact was uploaded on time but is not printed, place its digital
equivalent on the reserved display in the normal roster bay. If it missed noon,
the policies page sets the late consequence, but the defence still runs at
14:00. If you are absent, the plan is marked from the memo alone. Prints that
arrive after the hang closes have no bay, so be on the wall at 13:00.

## What is on the wall

Use the display set named by the assessment brief. Caption the simulation views
with in-game day and time. The 19:00 street-level observation also names the
issued fine-weather state, camera, polygon and one-minute count. A quiet plaza
is a finding; do not replace it with a better screenshot.

Pin the memo rather than leaving it on a laptop. Put the signed unfunded list
last, with its Week 8 lines intact. Every proposed public space carries a
monthly figure funded from your corridor budget sheet column. The evidence must
allow a reader to find the public-realm claim, its cost and what remained
unfunded without its author explaining the layout.

## How the jury runs

From 13:25, the cohort and jurors read the whole wall in silence for twenty
minutes. Do not stand beside your own sheets. At 14:00 the jury walks the first
eight plans in roster order, then the second eight after a recess. Each plan has
**four minutes**, with changeover inside the clock: ninety seconds for one claim
and the remainder for questions.

During the recess, Tobias Wren and Sunniva Marek choose three plans they
disagree about. Those return for six minutes each before the whole room. Marek
then gives fifteen uninterrupted minutes to the wall as a whole. The visiting
critic has not seen the Week 8 charrette or the explanations that made the work
seem settled at the desk. That distance is the point of their place in the
room.

## The question

Did the district improve because you designed something, or because you spent
money? Show the division.

The Ockley Green plaza costs **₡2,200/month** and the reference worksheet
associates four happiness points with it: ₡2,200 ÷ 4 = **₡550 per attributed
point**. Farrant Row’s street trees cost **₡340/month** and are associated with
three: ₡340 ÷ 3 ≈ **₡113 per attributed point**. Plaza, trees, paths and
maintenance changed together, so neither ratio is an isolated causal return.
Happiness rose nine points; the two unattributed points also need an account.

The harder question sits on the last sheet. The Farrant Row treatment increment
still costs ₡5,100/month and remains unfunded. The jury will ask where the memo
records the decision to spend ₡2,540 on public realm while leaving that line
open. The answer may defend either choice. It must show that the choice was
noticed.
