## What to bring

Open `<student ID>-w02` with the Brayhurst quarter zoned low-density
residential, its block dimension written in the save notes, and one dwelling
chosen by address. Bring last week’s readings, the 38%/24% Hessel Row pair and
the ₡310/month upkeep difference. Know the prevailing wind — WNW — and have the
wind view findable in seconds. Bring a sandbox for the comparison branch.

## What happens in the room

The first fifteen minutes treat the zoning palette as what it is: a use-based
code in which every painted cell names a use. Build Scheme A in the assessed
save, then Scheme B in the sandbox against the same block pattern and population
target.

Place and cost the fixed utilities, then read ground, air, water and noise
pollution as four separate views, followed by traffic volume, on both branches
at 08:00. The final board puts district and eastern-block land value beside
home-to-work distance and timed travel from the named front door. Return to
Scheme A before 15:35; Scheme B remains counterfactual evidence. The Assessment
1 brief is issued at 15:50.

## The two schemes

**Scheme A — separated.** Housing stays on the terrace, commerce forms a strip
on the Brayhurst collector, and industry goes to the **Pellow cells** on
Ombersley Flats. The cells are downwind, downstream, flat and cheap. Pollution
leaves the residential quarter, but the named dwelling sits about 3.2 km and
one river crossing from work, with one route carrying the trip.

**Scheme B — adjacent.** Mixed housing occupies the quarter’s main frontages
and corners; industry sits on the eastern edge of the Reach, about 0.8 km from
the dwelling, without a crossing and with several possible routes. The commute
contracts, while the pollution footprint lands on the eastern residential
blocks. Do not collapse ground stain, air footprint, receiving-water mark and
noise contour into one “plume”. The issue is where each medium lands relative
to wind and river flow, not whether one branch generates a more convenient
shape.

## The utilities pass

In Scheme A, place the water intake **upstream** of the Pellow outflow, place the
sewage outflow below the cells, and supply power at the district’s current
scale. Read the monthly cost once and reproduce the same positions in Scheme B.
This fixes a disclosed condition for the Week 7 stress test; it is not a second
design exercise or an Assessment 1 claim.

## What the readings said

The Scheme A reference row reaches **3,600 residents in 1,410 households** and
the first surplus, **+₡900/month**. Timed travel rises from 4.2 to **6.8
minutes**, district walk share falls from 31% to **22%**, happiness reaches 72%
and land value reaches 24. Transit remains 0%.

**Mobility down.** Nothing is queuing; the code simply put destinations further
apart. Strict separation lifted land value and balanced the books by lengthening
every trip. **A plan claiming all three metrics improved has not been read
carefully.**

## The tradeoff, and what you commit

This is **livability against mobility performance**. Measure both schemes from
the same front door, record the cost in the metric that loses, and commit Scheme
A to `<student ID>-w03`. Assessment 1 is due next Monday at 12:00, so a hybrid
district that buys both the crossing and the residential pollution footprint is
not a deferred decision. It is two costs without a position.
