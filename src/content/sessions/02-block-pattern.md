## What to bring

Bring the untouched `<student ID>-w01` archive and a working copy with the
1.2 km Sallow Head–Dunthorpe spur and an unzoned **Brayhurst quarter**. Lay the
quarter out at either 80 m or 160 m spacing and be able to state that dimension.
Bring the Hessel Row path-count table, frontage inventory, road-length reading,
08:00 volume screenshot and the close-one-link before-and-after figures.

Bring the numbers, not a description of the numbers. A student without the
path-count table starts on paper and misses the first desk-crit rotation.

## What happens in the room

At 13:10, each student has two minutes to show the unzoned layout and path-count
table, name the block dimension, and identify the least certain decision. The
room then tests redundancy on three projected layouts by counting independent
routes and reachable frontage.

At 14:20, load the issued populated pair: `kerrow-ref-w02-80` and
`kerrow-ref-w02-160`. Both use the same 480 × 480 m block on Hessel Row, the same
residential zoning and the same population target; only street spacing differs.
Use this pair for the controlled walk-share and upkeep comparison instead of
trying to delete, rebuild and settle two quarters inside the session.

Four small groups rotate through the walkability desk crit. The final board
records each committed dimension, the matching reference walk share and road
metres per dwelling. Before leaving, zone the personal quarter low-density
residential and commit one dimension in the save notes.

## The paired test

At 80 m spacing, the tested Hessel Row block has a **38% reference-scenario walk
share**. At 160 m it has **24%**. Short blocks let a pedestrian turn every 80 m,
so a walked route can approximate the straight line; long blocks force the same
trip around the perimeter.

The finer grid lays more road. Normalised against the same population, it costs
**₡310/month more per 1,000 residents in road upkeep**. Record road length and
occupied dwellings separately, divide them to derive road metres per dwelling,
and keep the economy-panel upkeep line as its own monetary reading. The
comparison is mobility bought with a recurring cost, not a preference between
two drawings.

## What the readings said

The worked reference district reaches **1,180 residents in 460 households**.
Its monthly balance is −₡1,900, its timed 08:00 route is 4.2 minutes, transit is
0%, district walk share is 31%, happiness is 68%, and the normalised land-value
score is 17. Do not confuse the district’s 31% with the tested block’s 38%.

**Fiscal down, mobility up.** The deficit narrowed because residents now pay
rates; that is growth, not evidence that the fine layout costs less. The layout
cost is the ₡310/month counterfactual carried per 1,000 residents. **A plan
claiming all three metrics improved has not been read carefully.**

## The one question

What is it actually like to walk here? Answer with one named front door, one
walked route and a number. If you propose a one-way conversion, another lane or
a new junction, bring the before-and-after reading that would justify it. A
layout defended as a picture is not ready for the desk.
