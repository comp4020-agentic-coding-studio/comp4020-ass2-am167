The twelve lectures are one argument in three movements. **Weeks 1–3** settle
what the ground will take and what separating uses costs you: a site, a block
dimension, a zoning scheme. **Weeks 4–6** put movement through that shape and
watch it fight back — density against the amenity radius, a transit spine
against its own subsidy, and a widened road that holds its travel-time saving
for three in-game weeks and then hands most of it back. **Weeks 7–12** stop
treating the district as a drawing and start treating it as a thing that has
to be paid for and defended: the utilities under it, a 22% budget cut, the
public realm bought out of that cut, a fire on the ridge Week 1 rejected, and
then Week 11, which turns the simulation around and asks what its own readouts
cannot see. Week 12 argues the whole semester as one plan.

No week introduces a metric the others do not use. The same three — fiscal
performance, mobility performance, livability — are read every week, and the
lecture's job each Monday is to name which two of them that afternoon's studio
will make you trade.

Twelve Monday lectures at 10:00, from 22 February to 24 May, with a two-week break after Week 6. Each prepares the afternoon studio: read that week’s brief and bring its named save or worksheet. Jury weeks require completed work before the noon upload.

The [resources and access guide](../resources/) explains teaching-room notices, lab access, worksheets and the evidence labels used in every deck. On phones, decks read as a continuous document; on desktop they retain the presentation view.
