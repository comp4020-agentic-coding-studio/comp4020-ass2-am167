# Studio policies

Every brief in this studio is a design problem. This page is not. It is the set
of rules the studio runs on, gathered in one place so that a brief can point at
a rule instead of restating it, and so that no rule arrives for the first time
in the week it costs you something.

Read it in Week 1, and again the night before your first pin-up.

Where a brief on the [assessment pages](/assessments/) sets a different rule for
one piece of work, that brief wins for that piece of work, and says so where it
does. Everything else here holds from Week 1 to the final jury.

## The pin-up deadline

A pin-up is not a submission portal that happens to close at noon. It is a room,
with a jury in it, booked for one afternoon. That is the entire reason the
deadline is a deadline, and it is why this studio does not treat "a day late" as
a small version of "on time".

Two things are due each time, and they run on different clocks.

**The artefact** --- your in-simulation screenshots and your defence memo --- is
due at 12:00 on the date named in the brief. Work uploaded after that loses five
percentage points per calendar day, weekends included, and is not marked at all
past five days. If you can see that coming, what you want is an
[extension](/policies/#extensions), not a penalty.

**The defence** happens at **14:00** the same day unless the brief names a
different start. It is heard by the week's studio tutor of record, your cohort,
and in assessed weeks a Visiting Critic who is in the building for that
afternoon and no other. The Course Convenor chairs Weeks 4 and 12; Week 9 is
chaired by the tutor of record. This one cannot be submitted late, because there
is nothing to submit it to. Miss the jury and your plan is marked from the memo
alone --- which means the memo has to answer, unprompted, every question the
room would have asked you. Most memos do not. Turn up.

Turning up matters in the weeks you are not being marked, too. A jury with no
audience is two people in a room looking at a screenshot, and the studio's
critique is the thing you are actually paying for. Attendance at pin-ups is
expected in all twelve [studio sessions](/sessions/).

## Extensions

Extensions are granted, and granted readily, when something outside your control
has eaten the time. Ask the Course Convenor in writing before the deadline. An
extension arranged in advance costs you nothing; a case made afterwards has to
argue about what you could have foreseen, which is a worse conversation for
everyone.

What an extension moves is the artefact clock. What it cannot move is a jury:
the room is booked, the Visiting Critic has a train to catch, and rescheduling
one student's fifteen minutes reschedules everyone's. In practice that means one
of two things, and you and the Convenor will pick between them when you ask:

- you present at the pin-up on the original date with whatever you have, and the
  extension applies to the artefact you upload afterwards; or
- you present at a desk crit with the Course Convenor alone, within a week, and
  forgo the wider room. Rescheduling one student's slot reschedules the room;
  that is why an extension cannot move the jury.

Neither is a penalty. They are what "extension" can actually mean in a format
where the deadline is other people's time.

A lost or corrupted save file is not, on its own, grounds for an extension ---
see [save discipline](/policies/#save-files-and-version-discipline) below. A
machine that has died and taken the save with it, when you were keeping the
backups the studio asks for, is.

## Authorship and what the crit is allowed to change

This studio has an unusual academic integrity problem, and it is worth naming it
directly rather than gesturing at the University's rules and hoping.

The whole method here is that other people tell you what is wrong with your
district and you go and change it. That is not a grey area at the edge of the
course; it is the course. So the line cannot be "your work must be uninfluenced
by others" --- nothing in a studio ever is. The line is authorship, and it sits
in three places.

**The district is yours.** One student, one save file, Week 1 to Week 12. You
may sit at another student's machine during a desk crit --- do, it is the
fastest way to learn a tool --- but you may not open, edit, or submit their
save, and they may not touch yours. A district built by two people is a
different assessment to the one everyone else sat.

**Advice taken is advice named.** Anything the room gave you that changed the
build gets a line in the defence memo: what was suggested, by whom, in which
week, and why you took it. This is not a confession. It is the ordinary
courtesy of design practice, and it is also the difference between a decision
you made and a decision that happened to you. A change you cannot account for
is one you did not really author, and the jury will find that out by asking.

**Everything imported is declared.** Asset packs, a road layout lifted from a
published plan, a district someone posted online, a real city's street grid
traced off a map --- all of it is fine, all of it is normal, none of it is
allowed to arrive silently. Name it in the memo and say what you did with it.

Passing off another person's design work as your own is plagiarism, and the
University's academic integrity rules apply here in full and unmodified. What
this section adds is the studio-specific reading of them.

## The standard map and simulation settings

Everyone in this studio builds on the same site: the standard map issued in
Week 1, on the settings listed in that session's brief. This is not
bureaucratic tidiness. The three metrics your work is marked against --- fiscal
performance, mobility performance and livability --- only mean something
comparatively, and they only compare if the ground underneath them is the same
ground.

So, for assessed work:

- **No simulation-altering mods.** Anything that changes traffic behaviour,
  demand, service radii, the economy or the cost of anything is out. A district
  that scores well on livability because a mod raised the ceiling has not scored
  well on livability.
- **Cosmetic mods and asset packs are fine**, and declared in the memo. How your
  district looks is your business; how it behaves is the studio's.
- **Unlimited money and unlock-all are off.** Fiscal performance is one of the
  three metrics; switching it off is not a design move.
- **Play the map you were issued.** Terrain is a constraint, and Week 1 is about
  reading it. Regenerating the site because you did not like the river is the
  one edit that invalidates every week that follows.

Sandbox saves, where you can do whatever you like to find out how a system
behaves, are encouraged and never marked. Keep them separate from the assessed
district.

## Save files and version discipline

Your district is a twelve-week argument, and the save file is the only record of
it. Treat it the way a studio treats drawings.

After every studio session, take a dated save named for your student ID and the
week --- `u1234567-w04` --- and put a copy somewhere that is not the machine you
built it on. Unassessed weeks also upload that save and the week's sheet to the
**course share issued in Week 1**, by **21:00 the same day**. A registered
access adjustment uses the next booked lab slot and emails the tutor before
21:00. Assessed artefacts follow the pin-up clock above, not this 21:00 line.
Week 1's session covers the routine.

Two things depend on this. The first is obvious: hardware fails, and the studio
cannot mark a district that no longer exists. The second is Week 11, where the
brief asks you to read your own district's land-value history from Week 1
forward. Students who kept the weekly saves have a semester of evidence for that
week. Students who kept one rolling save have a screenshot and a memory.

Submit the relevant save alongside the screenshots and memo each time the brief
asks for it.

## Generative tools

Use them. Research, reading, working out why the traffic AI is doing something
baffling, drafting and tightening the memo --- all reasonable, none of it
interesting to declare in detail.

What is not delegable is the design. The zoning call, the transit alignment, the
budget tradeoff and the reason you chose it over the obvious alternative are the
assessed work, and if a tool made those choices you have submitted its studio
project rather than yours.

One line in the defence memo naming the tools you used and what they did for you
is the whole disclosure requirement. Beyond that, the jury is a good detector:
a memo that argues for a decision its author cannot defend out loud reads
exactly the same whoever or whatever wrote it.

## Conduct at crit

Critique is the studio's core teaching method and the part most likely to go
wrong, so the room runs to a protocol:

1. The presenter presents, uninterrupted, for five minutes --- unless the brief
   for that pin-up names a different clock. Assessment 1 is six minutes (two
   presenting, four questions). Assessment 2 is a four-minute wall walk,
   changeover included. Assessment 3 is fifteen minutes from 13:00 in two rooms.
2. Clarifying questions only --- things you need to know to look at the work
   properly, not opinions with question marks on them.
3. Critique. Describe what you see before you judge it. Address the plan, not
   the planner.
4. The presenter responds last, and is not obliged to agree.

Nobody is required to defend their own competence, only their design decisions.
Comments about a student rather than a student's work end the crit, and the
University's code of conduct takes it from there.

If something in a crit lands badly --- yours or someone else's --- tell the
Course Convenor. It is a small studio and this is fixable when it is early.

## Equipment, access and adjustments

The studio runs on Cities: Skylines II, which needs a reasonably capable machine.
You do not need to own one. The design lab has **fifteen** machines with the
software installed and the standard map preloaded, available during studio hours
and bookable outside them; the cohort is **sixteen**. Bring-your-own is first.
Remaining students book lab machines; the sixteenth live save loads on the
**tutor station in Studio 2.14**, or as a booked pair on machine 15. Ordinary
weeks never require sixteen simultaneous lab saves. Jury weeks use the
presenting machines named in the brief, not sixteen live copies. The Week 1
session covers how.

If you have registered accessibility adjustments, they apply here without
argument or explanation. Some of the studio's own conventions have obvious
adjustments available --- presenting to the Course Convenor instead of a full
jury, additional time at the pin-up, a written defence in place of a spoken one,
a captioned recording rather than a live room. Raise it in Week 1 or 2 if you
can, so the adjustment is in place before the first assessed pin-up rather than
arranged around it.

Nothing in the pin-up format is more important than your being able to take part
in it.

## Where to ask what

| If it is about... | Ask |
| --- | --- |
| A brief, a mark, an extension, an adjustment | The Course Convenor, by email |
| The software, the map, a build that will not behave | Your tutor, during studio |
| Feedback on a specific design decision | Bring it to the desk crit --- that is what the hour is for |
| A lab machine, an account, a licence | The design lab technicians |
| Something that went wrong in a crit | The Course Convenor, as soon as you can |

Contact details for everyone named here are on the [people page](/people/). The
Visiting Critic does not take student email; their feedback arrives at the jury,
which is the point of having one.
